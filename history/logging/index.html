Logging history overview

Bly grew into a timber town through a mix of local mills, camp systems, and rail routes that carried logs and lumber out of the upper Sprague River country. Oral histories describe a steady rhythm of camp life, mill work, and rail activity through the mid-20th century, followed by a long shift as camps closed and mill-era work thinned out.

  • Early 1900s — Rail access and timber hauling expand in the upper Sprague River region.
  • 1930s — Logging camps and lumber work support families and schools in the Bly area.
  • 1940s — Multiple mills operate around Bly and Ivory Pine, shaping daily life and jobs.
  • 1950s–1960s — Weyerhaeuser-era logging expands; camp routines and rail hauling dominate.
  • 1973 — Camp life begins to fade as the stud mill era shifts travel and work patterns.
  • 1976–1990 — Sort yard period; mills decline and eventually disappear from Bly.
  • 1990s — Rail activity winds down as the logging economy shifts.
