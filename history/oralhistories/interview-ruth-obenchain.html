Interview with Ruth Obenchain, May 9, 2000

Interviewed by Eliza Buck, Lily Hevern and Gordon Hevern

Transcript text

Was born 4/26/12 born in Langell Valley Or.

Ruth's parents were Walter and Eva Campbell. They bought the Finley Ranch (Merkley ranch).

Children: Carolyn Obenchain, Margaret Jacobs. Carolyn's Children: Mike Madden, Eddie Madden, and Bill Proctor. Margaret's Children: Ryan (deceased) and Darrell.

In 1940 moved to Bly, Frank was husband's name.

I taught school and my husband was a rancher, he worked a short time at the mill, lived on the old Obenchain Ranch. He was born in Jacksonville, Eva, parents lived there.

I have 8 great grandchildren, Erin and Becky, Bridie and Frank. Morgan, Amanda, Makayla, Ashley.

We moved here in 1940, got married in 1933. I began teaching school in 1931, taught at Henley, Sprague River, Altamont, married women caused a commotion because of women taking men's jobs. I taught in a lumber camp near Hildebrand, 9 kids to begin and down to 5. That was quite a winter. Logging train went through so I rode the train and then walked 2.5 miles. I could catch the train at the switchback. I also taught music at Bonanza. Superintendent said if you could go to Bly he would hire another at Bonanza. I started in Bly in 1937. It was a very nice experience. I didn't have to be tough they knew what I expected.

I taught 5th and 6th and then High school, then 7 & 8th; I was the principal the last 7 years.

They didn't want to put single women in the lumber camp so they sent me. They had pinochle parties and the folks were nice. Logging camp wages was $100/month for the first year, next year depression $90 but did their own janitor for $7.00 per month. They closed early because they didn't want to give bonds. I lived in one of the cabins. It was two sheds together; I kept part of it shut off in the winter. It had a small woodstove. Roads would get muddy. There was lots of complaint about women working when the men couldn't get jobs.

I quit teaching in 1970. I was thankful to have a job. I thought everyone should be a teacher.

There used to be a school on Campbell road. Just near the highway on the knoll. I don't know the name. I remember the Boyd children went to play in the old building.

Ranch has been added on too. Pastureland, 640 acres, added Anderson Field. Traded land with the BK ranch. She thinks it is about 2300 acres. Not all pasture land. Raised hay for the cattle, have to buy hay. They have about 300 head of cattle. Carolyn and Margaret with the help of Mike run the ranch.

We took baths in the washtub. We had a big carbide tank and carbide lights. Ruth's folks bought a new Delco plant so they got the old one. They and the BK ranch put in electricity in 1951. Margaret was born in 1951.

I enjoy having family close. We mostly took working vacations.

Played at card parties, 500 and Pedro, had dances. Not enough people left to play bridge now.

When I went to camp, I had 22 Colt pistol, on a nail up above the bed. People next door ask her to pinochle party. The man was drinking, old southern couple. Got pretty rough and their son walked her home. I got the pistol down and kept it beside my bed. Many of the loggers came from the South because there wasn't any work there. There were lots of people from Arkansas. There was a Pelican Bay Camp. Euwana Camp, that employed lots of people. We had 3 main mills with 2 smaller at one time. There was a big Weyerhaeuser camp up North. The school bus was so loaded that the kids at Ivory Pine had to be picked up by the Sprague River bus.

When I went to high school, there were just 3 people. County paid the money towards their board. That was in 1925 or 26. Ruth was sent to Eugene to a boarding school and then to a University High School later. Bly re-opened the year Ruth came to teach in 1937. First school burned down. There were two rooms to start with and they gradually added on to. Did an EPA build the back part? It was already built on the other end. Gym was built in around 1938 or 39. The high school is where the medical center now sits. It closed in about 1964. They sent kids to Bonanza or Lakeview. It was too expensive because they had to pay out of county tuition when they chose Lakeview. Now everyone has to attend Bonanza.

The Gearhart School grades K-8th are in Bly. Called a 6/6 plan. We never had to wear uniforms. I taught freshman and junior subjects one year and the next sophomore and senior. We didn't have enough people. I didn't teach the year Carolyn was born and Margaret was born during the 3 years I quit. They had a hard time getting teachers to come to Bly.

I had surgery on throat and can't sing now.

I taught 5th and 6th standard classes plus music. They don't have history now it is called Social Studies. In the 8th grade we had 6 weeks of Oregon history; it is not taught anymore. General high school had algebra and geometry, didn't have foreign languages, I taught English, history and Math and Home Economics also. I had sewing at University.

The logging camp was taught required grade school subjects. The hardest thing was I was not a primary teacher. How would you compare with today's teaching? Don't ask. I didn't often assign homework; most kids got it done at school.

Logging camps usually only had about 6 families. Weyerhaeuser was here a long time. When it closed it hurt the community. Ivory Pine had a cookhouse etc. and it was a large community.

We had 3 grocery stores, a drug store, and a clothing store about where Granville Curtis lives. The only building in 1920 is the old brick building where Leonardo has the antique store. The rest of them are gone. Most burned or were torn down.

The Dixon Place where Valladaos live here used to be a two story house; Dixon cut the top off of the building.

Dave Campbell bought the ranch and moved here from Langell Valley.

PHOTO: Right hand side was built on for a high school. Assembly of God church used to be a Methodist church. Hazel Peters owned the house in photo. Givens daughter had 4th grade in a separate building. Ruth and Frank lived in a house that was partially finished. It had no steps so Frank would pull her up. Tower picture KFPA. (Where the library sits now) Man that was head of the CCC camp lived in Steve Cornell's house. School playground used to be the CCC campsite. Men came in from all over the U.S. Civilian Conservation Corps, got money for it but I don't think they paid much. The Sprague River Park was built by the CCCs.

When I moved here in 1920 I rode horse back to school. We had a little barn behind, Dave Campbell hauled hay but no one else would. Hamaker had a barn and kept the horses there one year. There was a big barn in the back of where Nisha Hadley now lives. Best place for a garden. Across the highway, huge big barn, hotel and stage stop, about where the liquor store is now. Change of horses and all winter the only transportation was horse. I had a brother that died at age 16 appendix attack and it was winter, took him in a hack, past Dairy, had gang green and back then they didn't have sulfur drugs.

The Arther ranch, down the mountain around the bend, kept people at their house. Stagecoach went out by the dump and over the mountain to Bonanza. They made a left turn at Beatty around the hill to Whiskey Creek. We got stuck in the old Ford many times.

Personal experiences? Frank got thrown a few times. Dave Campbell got badly hurt when he tried to stop a team; broke a hip.

The Obenchain ranch was formally owned by Givens and Jack Watts before that. Watts mother was a Casebeer. Frank bought from Givens.

Ranching: Summer irrigates and raises hay, cut and raked and baled. Haul and stack. Feed is gone by Thanksgiving and you have to begin feeding the cattle. Push hay from a trailer. We keep blocks of salt out. Calving and branding. Many cows have trouble and need help. Sometimes we have to feed calves with bottles. Little calves about 6 weeks have to be branded and ear marked. Earmarks are registered just like brands. Some people used a waddle. Have to have shots for black leg. This county is low in selenium, have to have a shot. Girls check everyday for new calves, give shots. It is hard work and lots of expense. Environmentalists and Indians are trying to take the water away. Assessed for every irrigated land, someone has to pay. Indians do not.

What would happen if all the water rights were taken away? No idea what we would do. Just take a header for the river. Scary to think, couldn't drill a well. We would need water rights for that too. The meadows would dry up and it would put people out of business. Hope some of those that started this starve to death. It started 4-5 years ago. People have felt sorry for the Indians, robbed by the white man, they were paid for the reservation and now they want the reservation back. We have a small allotment and rent some pasture above us. Forest Service and the environmentalist wants the forest cattle free. They force people to fence all the streams to keep the cows out.

Cows make paths down to the water etc. Cows naturally come in the fall. Some have to be brought in. I don't think the cattle damage the forest. Maybe re-plant with the small trees.

Carolyn got tipped over on a three-wheeler one time trying to get to a new calf but now have quad runners. Carolyn and Margaret are cowgirls, just ranchers and they do everything. Buckaroo and cowboy? There are some cowboys still around. Bly cowboy hats shaped different. They dress less fancy and many wear baseball caps instead of western hats. Always wore big hats when Ruth was a kid.

Some have milk cows, Jersey, Guernsey, and Holsteins. Hereford and Durham, Angus are used for meat. Holstein has stronger feet. We went to Colorado to buy Holstein cattle. We would pick out good bulls and hope they inherit the traits. You can get scrawny cattle if you don't watch. It is hard to keep a good herd up. Good bull costs about $4000. We keep separate until you want them to calve. Usually the weather is pretty good in February. We sell in October. It takes about 10 or 11 bulls to service the whole herd. We usually keep them for 3 years and then sell. Sometimes calves are too big and need help being born. Sometimes you lose the calf and sometimes the cow. Lose one there goes a whole year. Calves are sold at about 9 month. Slaughter at 1000 pounds or so.

One day it was sleeting out and I asked Carolyn if there wasn't another way to make a living and she said, "Well, I wouldn't be anywhere else". We love ranching, out in the open, but you can't go anywhere. Always have to be someone there watching for scours, lung trouble etc. I cooked for the crew while the other women helped with the branding. Branding day, 10-12 is all. Campbell ranch has a big crew all year round. Can't hire people for the wage they can make everywhere else. Have old equipment but don't owe anything. We start baling after the 4th of July. Baled hay stores easier than the shocks people used to leave in the fields or store in the barn. Dave Campbell built a large hay slide. They would slide under the shocks of hay, yarded to the nets and fastened a cable and the derrick driver pulled it up. It made a good-looking solid stack. Rain ruins the hay. Hay in the barn is like money in the bank.

It was all ranchers when I moved here then logging came in. Young guys worked on the ranches. What grew to be a logging town, started out as a stage stop. Ollie Howard had the hotel and barn.

Breaking horses? We don't do that anymore. We don't ride enough to keep them tame. We have two horses left. Carolyn can't ride anymore. The fences are expensive to put up and maintain.

Are there rodeos around? Not a lot. They have a big one in Lakeview. Oldest, started in Bly at Parker Flat 1921, or 22; had a grandstand and everything. It got too large and they moved to Klamath Falls.

Were there work parties, barn raising? Not usually here. But everyone came for brandings. Now we have to do with the kids when they are available. Ted Hyde and Bill Hill used to come help Frank. It is easier to do with more people.

Secret recipes? I was not a camp cook and just cooked at home. I always baked baking powder biscuits. Carolyn would say "Mom, bake biscuits." I always had dessert. Vanilla cream pie and berry cobbler, chocolate upside down cake were my specialties.

The Ruth Obenchain award is given at the elementary school each year to a deserving girl of the 8th grade. No money, just recognition. The men's award is the Mike Mansfield goes to a boy. Mike drowned in the Gerber Reservoir.

We drove a buggy to school; one year another girl would drive a buggy. There was a man that used to live in Bailey Flat, Sunny Jim, had a beard, and we were scared of him. Don't know why we were afraid. Dad hired him to work later at the ranch.

My legs don't work too well but they work. Betty Patzke was at our church on Sunday we used to play basketball. I don't believe we could do it now. One of Betty's sister lives in Klamath Falls is crippled up.

We have a new minister at our church; the CMA. We have not had a minister for a while. The church has been there, rebuilt after being burned. Nice church, before Weyerhaeuser left we had to build on. The Forest Service mostly moved out.

I met Frank when I was in the 3rd grade. After I was in college he asked me to go to a dance. Just past Nisha's house there was a community hall. We played cards, basketball, danced and etc. The big kids were pretty rough on us. Mr. Parker played the fiddle; later we had bands that came out. People came because that was all there was to do. It was later turned into a movie theater. Build new theater? That came in after I began teaching. They borrowed Dad's cement mixer. I think the first movie played was there was Bambi. The last one was Rufus J. Pickle and the Indian. Harry Obenchain was here before I was.

Laboree home was called the Haunted House. I came home at Christmas and went to a dance over there. They had a fire in the fireplace. Van Landrum gave me a picture of it. Didn't Ethel Fairchild's first husband own part of the Laboree place? Laboree's wife wore the jewels. She lost everything, had a sale, and bought inlaid linoleum. It had to be scrubbed and she let the chickens in. Man quite well to do in the railroad business. She was a dance hall girl and after he died she married Elmer Lowery. They lived at the big barn, my folks not good at gossip. His wife came here and married Vance Hall. I knew him. She was dance hall girl from New York. He died and the lawyers ended up with most of the money. Liskey bought from the Indians. People sent history on the Laboree place.

Drew and then Dave would ride the colts. Both brothers were good horsemen. Drew died when he was 16.
